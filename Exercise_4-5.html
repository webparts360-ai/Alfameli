Exercise 4-5: HR Pulse Survey Analysis

Time
14 minutes
Tool
Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat, signed in with your work account
You'll produce
A verified theme table, a confidential follow-up note for HR, and a management report with themes, frequency, sentiment, insights and recommendations

All names, companies and comments are fabricated for training purposes.

The job

You're in HR. The monthly pulse survey has closed with 30 free-text comments. The HR Manager needs a one-page summary for Thursday's management meeting. Next month there'll be another 30, and the month after.

Reading, grouping and counting comments by hand takes an hour. AI does it in seconds. But survey comments are about feelings, not facts, and that creates new ways for AI to get it wrong: the loudest comment wins, quotes get tidied up, and the one comment that really matters gets buried in a theme.

New techniques in this exercise:

TechniqueWhat it means
Data sensitivity checkStop and check before pasting personal or sensitive information into any AI tool.
Evidence rulesEvery claim needs proof: a count, the respondent numbers, and quotes word for word.
Exception promptingAsk what doesn't fit the summary, so the unusual comment isn't lost among the common ones.
Splitting by audienceOne analysis, two outputs: a confidential note for HR, and a report for management.
Finding, insight, actionSeparate what the data shows, what it means, and what to do about it, so opinions can't pass as facts.

  • Company policy allows this kind of data in this tool. If I'm not sure, I ask first.

3Prompt 2: the critical prompt, theme analysis (4 minutes)

This is the one you build yourself. Use RCTF+C, and put your evidence rules in the Format and Constraints lines.

Everything after this is built on this table, so get it right here.

1. Copy this skeleton into Copilot Chat. Don't send it yet.

RCTF+C skeleton
Role: You are a ...
Context: ...
Task: ...
Format: ...
Constraints: ...

2. Replace each "..." with your own words.

LineAsk yourself
RoleWho normally analyses survey feedback?
ContextWhat was the survey question? What are you pasting in?
TaskWhat do you want it to do with the comments?
FormatWhat columns would let you check the result? Think: how many, which respondents, what they said. What order should themes be in? Should the counts add up to something?
ConstraintsYour evidence rules. Can it tidy up quotes? What about the Bahasa Malaysia comments? What about department names and identifying details? Should strong wording affect the ranking?

3. Paste Dataset B underneath your prompt, then send.

4. Check the table:

  • Do the counts add up to 30? Is any comment missing, or counted twice?
  • Pick one theme. Check its respondent numbers against Dataset B. Is the count right?
  • Search for every quote in Dataset B (Ctrl+F). Is each one word for word?
  • Were the Bahasa Malaysia comments counted and translated correctly?
  • Is the order based on counts, or on how angry the comments sound?
  • Could any quote identify someone?
  • Compare with your gut feel from Step 2. What surprised you?

If something is wrong, fix it with Draft, Diagnose, Direct:

Example critical prompt
Role: You are an HR analyst summarising employee survey feedback.
Context: Below are 30 comments from our October pulse survey. The question was: "What is one thing we should improve, and why?"
Task: Group the comments into themes.
Format: A table with these columns: Theme | Number of comments | Respondent numbers | Two quotes. Order the themes by number of comments, highest first. Every comment must appear in exactly one theme, and the counts must add up to 30.
Constraints: Quote comments word for word. Never tidy, shorten or paraphrase inside quotation marks. Quote Bahasa Malaysia comments exactly as written, with an English translation in brackets. Do not include department names with quotes, and leave out any quote that could identify a person. Base the order on counts, not on how strongly something is worded. British spelling.

4What doesn't fit? (1.5 minutes)

A theme table shows what's common. It can hide what's serious. Ask for the exceptions.

Quick promptPrompt 3: exception prompting

Prompt 3
Which comments didn't fit neatly into any theme? Then list any comment that suggests a safety, conduct or wellbeing concern that needs individual follow-up rather than being summarised. Quote each one exactly, give the respondent number, and say why.
Check before moving on
  • Read every comment it flags. Do you agree each one needs individual follow-up?
  • Did it miss any? Compare with what worried you in Step 2.
  • Where were these comments hiding in the theme table?
Hint

Serious concerns often appear at the end of an ordinary complaint. Reread the last sentence of the longer comments.

5Confidential note for HR (1 minute)

Deal with the serious comments first, and keep them away from the management report.

Quick promptPrompt 4: the confidential note

Prompt 4
Using only the comments you flagged for individual follow-up, write a short confidential note to the HR Manager. For each one, give the respondent number, quote the comment exactly, and say why it needs individual follow-up rather than being included in a summary. Keep it factual and do not speculate about who is involved.
Check before moving on
  • Is each comment quoted exactly, with its respondent number?
  • Does the note stick to facts, without guessing who is involved?

6Management report (2 minutes)

Now chain from your verified table to the report management will actually read. This time you write the prompt.

Your promptPrompt 5: the management report

What management expects (two pages at most):

SectionWhat it should show
HeadlineThe overall picture in a few sentences
ThemesEach theme with how many people raised it, the percentage, the sentiment and the average satisfaction score
InsightsFor the top themes: what the data shows, what it means for the business, and the evidence
RecommendationsA short, ranked list, each with an owner and a first step
LimitsWhat this survey can't tell us

And it must: rank by the numbers, not by tone; show what's fact and what's interpretation; and leave out anything confidential or identifying.

Tip: write it as a short RCTF+C prompt. Refer to "the verified theme table above" and the scores in Dataset B instead of pasting data again.

Check before moving on
  • Do the percentages add up to about 100%?
  • Pick one theme. Work out its average satisfaction score yourself from Dataset B. Does it match?
  • Does the sentiment for each theme match what people actually wrote?
  • Is every insight backed by a count and a real quote? Is anything presented as fact that is really an opinion?
  • Look at the recommendation ranking. Does it reflect how many people are affected, how badly, or both? Is a small, very unhappy group given the right priority?
  • Does the "can't tell us" section make sense for a 30-person monthly survey?
  • Is anything from the confidential note, or anything identifying, in the report?
  • Does the report meet the quality requirements you wrote in Step 1?

Example report prompt
Using only the verified theme table above and the satisfaction scores in Dataset B, write a management report for Thursday's meeting with these sections:

1. Headline: three sentences on the overall picture.
2. Theme overview: a table with Theme | Number of comments | % of the 30 responses | Overall sentiment (positive, mixed or negative) | Average satisfaction score for that theme's respondents. Show the respondent numbers used for each average.
3. Key insights: for each of the top four themes, give the Finding (what the data shows), the Insight (what it means for the business) and the Evidence (the count and one short quote, word for word).
4. Recommendations: up to five, ranked by how many people are affected and how serious the issue is. For each, suggest an owner and a first step.
5. What this survey can't tell us: the limits of this data.

Rules: rank by the numbers, not by how strongly comments are worded. Label anything that is interpretation rather than fact. Do not include anything from the confidential note, or anything that could identify anyone. British spelling. No more than two pages.
Optional: finish in Word

Copy the report into Word. If you have Copilot in Word, ask it to format the report with headings and a clean table, keeping every figure and quote exactly as it is.

If you get stuck
  1. Read the report as a manager who wasn't in the survey. What would they think the biggest problem is? Is that right?
  2. Read it as the person who wrote each flagged comment. Would they feel safe?
  3. Which Module 1 technique checks every fact before you sign it off? Apply it to the counts, averages and quotes.

7Check your work (1 minute)

  • I ran the "before you paste" check.
  • My theme counts add up to 30, and I checked at least one theme's respondent numbers.
  • Every quote is word for word, including the Bahasa Malaysia comments.
  • Themes are ranked by how many people raised them, not by how loudly.
  • I asked what didn't fit, and every comment needing follow-up is in the confidential note.
  • Nothing in the management report could identify anyone, or reveal the confidential note.
  • I checked at least one average satisfaction score myself.
  • Every insight in the report is backed by a count and a real quote.
  • The report meets the quality requirements I set in Step 1.

Ticked fewer than seven? Go back to the checks under Prompt 2.

What to take away

  • Check before you paste. The more personal the data, the more it matters which tool you use.
  • Counts keep AI honest. AI notices strong feelings. Only counts show how many people share them.
  • Quotes are evidence. A tidied-up quote is no longer the employee's voice.
  • Ask what doesn't fit. Summaries can bury the one comment that matters most.
  • Some things aren't themes. A safety or conduct concern goes to a person, not into a percentage.
  • In HR, anonymity is part of accuracy. A correct quote that identifies someone is still a failure.
  • Finding, insight, action. Keep what the data shows separate from what you think it means.
  • How many and how badly. Frequency and sentiment together tell you what to fix first.
