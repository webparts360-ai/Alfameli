Exercise 4-4: Procurement Quote Comparison

Time
12 minutes
Tool
Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat, then Excel (optional)
You'll produce
A quote comparison table, and a shortlist with risks, outliers and follow-up questions

All names, companies and details are fabricated for training purposes.

The job

You're a procurement officer. You sent an RFQ for gloves, safety boots and coveralls. Six vendors have replied, every one in a different format. Your manager wants them in one comparison table by lunch.

Retyping six messy replies into a table is boring, slow and easy to get wrong. It's exactly the kind of work AI is best at, if you tell it precisely what you want and check what it gives you.

New prompting techniques in this exercise:

TechniqueWhat it means
Schema promptingTell the AI the exact columns, their order, and the allowed values. It fills in your structure instead of inventing its own.
Rules for messy dataTell it what to do when information is missing, unclear or in a different unit, so it flags instead of guessing.
Prompt chainingBreak the job into stages. Check the output of one stage, then build the next stage on it, so the AI works from verified data, not raw input.
Spot-check samplingYou can't check every row of bulk output. Pick a few at random and trace them to the source.
Terms used in this exercise
  • RFQ: request for quotation
  • SST: sales and service tax
  • Validity: how long the vendor will hold their price
  • Minimum order: the smallest quantity a vendor will sell at that price

Prompt 1

Your task: decide what your comparison table should look like, and what the AI should do when a reply is messy. The AI will follow your design, so this is where the quality comes from.

QuestionMy decision
What columns do I need, in what order?
What should it write when something is missing?
What should it do when vendors use different units, or treat tax differently?
What should it flag for me to follow up?
Hints if you're stuck
  • Compare units. Is every vendor pricing the same thing? Look closely at gloves.
  • Compare dates. When does each quote expire? Is any already out of date?
  • Look for what's missing. Which replies don't answer everything the RFQ asked?
  • Look for conditions. Does any price only apply if we do something, or buy a certain amount?
  • Decide about calculations. Should the AI convert or calculate anything, or just copy what the vendor wrote? What's the risk either way?

3Prompt 2: the critical prompt, extraction (4 minutes)

This is the one you build yourself. Use RCTF+C, and put your Step 2 design into it.

Build it carefully. In Step 4, the recommendation is built on this table, so any mistake here carries through.

1. Copy this skeleton into Copilot Chat. Don't send it yet.

RCTF+C skeleton
Role: You are a ...
Context: ...
Task: ...
Format: ...
Constraints: ...

2. Replace each "..." with your own words.

LineAsk yourself
RoleWho normally builds quote comparisons?
ContextWhat will you paste in? The RFQ and the replies.
TaskWhat do you want back: just a table, or follow-up questions too?
FormatThis is the schema. List your exact columns in order. How many rows per vendor? What values are allowed in each column?
ConstraintsThese are your rules for messy data, from Step 2. Missing information? Different units or tax? Calculations? Guessing?

3. Paste the RFQ and Dataset B underneath your prompt, then send.

4. Spot-check. You can't check every row of a big table, so sample it:

  • Pick two vendors at random. Check every figure in their rows against their reply in Dataset B.
  • Is every "Not stated" genuinely missing from the reply?
  • Did it copy each price exactly, or did it convert or calculate anything?
  • Is every row that can't be compared like-for-like flagged?

If something is wrong, don't start again. Fix it with Draft, Diagnose, Direct:

Correction template
In your [table / recommendation], [what is wrong]. Change it to [what it should be], and keep everything else the same.
Stuck or out of time? Show an example critical prompt

Only open this after you've written your own, then compare.

Example critical prompt
Role: You are a procurement officer preparing a quote comparison.
Context: Below are the RFQ we sent and the replies from six vendors.
Task: Extract every quote into one comparison table, then list the follow-up questions for each vendor.
Format: One row per vendor per item, with these columns in this order: Vendor | Item | Price as quoted | Unit as quoted | SST (Included / Excluded / Not stated) | Delivery as quoted | Valid until | Flags. After the table, a short list of follow-up questions for each vendor.
Constraints: Copy every figure exactly as the vendor wrote it. Do not convert units, currency or tax, and do not calculate anything. Write "Not stated" for anything missing and never guess. In Flags, note anything that stops a like-for-like comparison, such as a different unit, tax treatment, minimum order, partial quote, expired or missing validity, or a lump sum. British spelling.

[paste the RFQ]
[paste Dataset B]
Optional: move the table into Excel

Copy the table from Copilot Chat and paste it into a blank Excel sheet. If you have Copilot in Excel, ask it to highlight every row with a flag. Excel is where you'd sort, filter and total the prices later.

4Chain to a recommendation (2 minutes)

Your table is checked. Now build on it. Instead of asking the AI to read six messy replies and recommend in one go, you ask it to work only from the table you've already verified. That's prompt chaining.

Send this in the same chat:

Quick promptPrompt 3: from table to recommendation

Prompt 3
Using only the table above, check each vendor against every requirement in the RFQ and mark it Pass, Fail or Unclear. Then shortlist up to two vendors with reasons, and list the risks and outliers. Where you need to convert a price to compare it, show the working in a separate column. The final decision is mine.
Check before moving on
  • Does the shortlist rely on anything that isn't in your table?
  • Pick one price conversion and check the working yourself.
  • Is every quote that can't be compared like-for-like excluded or flagged, rather than quietly ranked?
  • How many vendors actually pass every requirement? What does that tell you about the next step?
  • Did it leave the final decision with you?
If you get stuck
  1. Read the shortlist as your manager would. What's the first question they'd ask?
  2. Go back to your Step 2 decisions. Does the recommendation respect every rule you set?
  3. Which Module 1 technique checks every fact before you sign it off? Apply it to the recommendation.

5Check your work (1 minute)

  • My prompt set exact columns, in a fixed order.
  • My prompt told the AI what to do with missing or unclear information.
  • Every price in my table is exactly as the vendor quoted it.
  • Every quote that can't be compared like-for-like is flagged.
  • I spot-checked at least two vendors against their replies.
  • I checked the table before asking for a recommendation.
  • I checked at least one price conversion in the recommendation myself.
  • The final decision stayed with me.

Ticked fewer than five? Go back to the checks under Prompt 2.

What to take away

  • Give the AI your structure. A schema turns messy replies into a table you can trust and compare.
  • Rules beat hope. Tell it what to do with missing, unclear or different data, or it will decide for you.
  • Copy first, calculate later. Keep extraction and calculation separate, so you can check each.
  • Chain, don't cram. Extract, check, then recommend. Each stage builds on output you've already verified.
  • Sample bulk output. Spot-check a few rows at random, every time.
  • AI shortlists, you decide. A recommendation is input to your decision, not the decision.
