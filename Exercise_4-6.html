The job

You're in cost control. Every month you compare actual site costs against budget, work out the variances, and flag the lines that need explaining. You've just inherited the workbook from a colleague who has moved to another project.

So far today, you've asked AI for answers. With numbers, that's risky: AI can get sums wrong, and a number typed into a cell can't be checked. This time you'll ask AI to build the tool, and let Excel do the maths.

New ways to use AI in this exercise:

TechniqueWhat it means
Ask for the method, not the resultAsk AI for the Excel formulas instead of the numbers. Excel calculates, every month, and anyone can check how.
AI as explainerAsk AI to explain a formula you didn't write, in plain English, and to spot what's hardcoded or will break.
Test one row by handBefore trusting a formula across the sheet, check one row with a calculator.

From: Siti (Finance Manager)
Subject: October site cost variance report

Hi,

Please update the site cost report for October. For each cost line I need:

- The variance against budget in RM and in %
- Whether it's over or under budget
- A flag on any line that needs an explanation: more than 5% AND more than RM 2,000 away from budget, over or under

I'll reuse this every month, so please make sure it works without anyone retyping numbers. Also, Amir built the full-year forecast column before he moved to the Kuantan project, and I'm not sure anyone understands it. Can you check it?

Siti

Example critical prompt
Role: You are a financial analyst who builds clear, reusable Excel reports.
Context: My sheet has cost categories in A5:A11, monthly budget in column B, monthly actual in column C, and a total row in row 12. Finance wants each line's variance in RM and %, whether it is over or under budget, and a flag on lines more than 5% AND more than RM 2,000 away from budget, in either direction.
Task: Give me the Excel formulas for three new columns, F, G and H, starting in row 5, and a formula to replace anything in the total row that should be calculated.
Format: For each column: the header, the formula for row 5, how to fill it down, and one sentence explaining what it does.
Constraints: Give formulas only. Do not calculate any results yourself. Apply the flag rule exactly as written, with both conditions. For costs, over budget is unfavourable, so label the direction in words. Handle a zero budget without an error. British spelling.

4Explain the inherited formula (2 minutes)

Amir's forecast column is now yours. Before you rely on it, find out what it actually does.

Quick promptPrompt 3: AI as explainer

Prompt 3
Explain this Excel formula in plain English, step by step: =D5/9*12
It's in the "Full-year forecast" column of our October 2026 site cost report. Column D is "Actual YTD (Jan to Oct, RM)".
Point out anything hardcoded, anything that is wrong this month, and anything that will break next month. Then suggest a more reliable version.
Check before moving on
  • Do you now understand every part of the formula?
  • Is the current forecast too high, too low, or right? By roughly how much?
  • Would the suggested version still work in November without anyone editing it?
  • Try the suggested version on the Fuel row. Does the result make sense?

With Copilot in Excel: select cell E5 and ask Copilot to explain the formula. It reads the sheet itself, so you don't need to describe the columns.

If you get stuck
  1. Look at the column D header. How many months of spending does it contain?
  2. What does the 9 in the formula represent? Is that right for October?
  3. What would have to change every month for this formula to stay right?

What to take away

  • Ask for the method, not the result. A formula can be checked and reused. A number from a chat can't.
  • Excel does the maths, AI does the explaining. Use each for what it's good at.
  • Describe your sheet. AI can't see your workbook unless it's working inside it, so tell it where everything is.
  • Test one row by hand. A formula that's wrong in one row is wrong in every row.
  • Inherited spreadsheets hide assumptions. Ask AI to explain them before you rely on them.
  • No typed totals. If it can be calculated, it should be a formula.
