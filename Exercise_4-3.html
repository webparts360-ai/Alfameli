Exercise 4-3: Shift and Site Coordination

Time
12 minutes
Tool
Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat (optional finish in Copilot in Teams)
You'll produce
A decision on two shift swap requests, an updated roster, and a message for each person affected

All names, companies and details are fabricated for training purposes.

The job

You're a site coordinator. Two crew members have asked to swap shifts. Saying yes takes ten seconds. Finding out afterwards that the swap left the site short of a key role takes much longer to fix.

The job is not just to update the roster. It's to test each change before you approve it, then tell everyone affected clearly.

This exercise introduces a new way to use AI: as a scenario tester that asks "if we do this, what breaks?"

RoleWhat the AI does
TeacherWorks out exactly what each person is asking for
Scenario testerPlays out each change against the roster and site rules
CoordinatorProduces the decision, the updated roster and the messages
CriticReviews your decision as the approver would

You stay the decision-maker. Read everything the AI gives you before moving on.

Terms used in this exercise
  • 14/14 rotation: 14 days on site, then 14 days off. 28/28 is the same in 28-day blocks.
  • Onsite / Offsite: whether the person is currently working on site or on their break.
  • Crew change: the day people arrive on site and others leave.
  • Back-to-back partner: the person on the opposite rotation who covers your role while you're off site.

Hints if you're stuck
  • Count by role. For each request, count how many people in each role would be on site after the change. Compare with rule 2.
  • Check the roles. Look up each person's role in the roster, not just their name.
  • Count the days. If someone stays on longer, how many days beyond their normal rotation would that be?
  • Follow the partners. When someone leaves on crew change day, who normally takes over? What happens if that changes?
  • Check who has spoken. A swap involves two people. Have both of them asked for it?
  • Check the clock. When is the crew change, and when would approval be needed by?

Quick promptPrompt 2: AI as scenario tester (1.5 minutes)

Copy the prompt, then paste Dataset B and Dataset C underneath it in the same message, and send.

Prompt 2
Here is our current roster and our site rules. For each swap request, play out what would happen if we approved it exactly as asked. Check the result against every site rule and tell me which rules pass and which fail. Before you conclude, ask me about anything you need to know.

Answer the AI's questions honestly. Only use what's in the datasets. If you don't know, copy this:

Reply for an unknown
I don't know that yet. Don't assume an answer. Treat it as something I need to find out, and tell me who I should ask.
Check before moving on
  • Compare the AI's results with your Step 2 table. Did it find the same rule problems? Did it find any you missed?
  • Did it assume anyone had agreed to something they haven't actually said?
  • Did it check every rule, including the ones that pass?

3Prompt 3: the critical prompt (3 minutes)

This is the one you build yourself. Use RCTF+C from Module 2.

This prompt produces your decision, the updated roster and the messages people will read. Fill in every line.

1. Copy this skeleton into Copilot Chat. Don't send it yet.

RCTF+C skeleton
Role: You are a ...
Context: ...
Task: ...
Format: ...
Constraints: ...

2. Replace each "..." with your own words.

LineAsk yourself
RoleWho normally handles shift swap requests? Ask the AI to act as that person.
ContextThe AI already has the requests, roster, rules and your answers in this chat. Refer to them.
TaskWhat do you need for each request? Think: a decision, what happens to the roster, and who needs to be told.
FormatHow should the decision be shown so the approver can check it quickly? What does the updated roster look like? Each person gets their own message: should an email reply read the same as a WhatsApp reply?
ConstraintsWhat should the AI do if a question is still unanswered? How will you stop it assuming someone has agreed? How will you make it show which rule supports each decision? What must no message promise yet? Spelling?

3. Before you press enter, check:

  • All five lines are filled in.
  • The Task asks for a decision, the roster change and the messages.
  • The Format gives each person their own message, suited to how they contacted us.
  • The Constraints stop the AI approving anything that still has an open question.
  • The Constraints make the AI name the rule behind each decision.

4. Check the output before moving on:

  • Does the output meet the quality checklist you wrote in Step 1?
  • After the changes, count each role on site. Does every role still meet rule 2?
  • For every approved change, has everyone involved actually agreed?
  • Does each decision name the rule it relies on? Is that rule really in Dataset C?
  • Does any message promise something that hasn't been approved yet?
  • Read one message as the person receiving it. Would they know exactly what happens next?

If something is wrong, don't start again. Tell the AI exactly what to fix (Draft, Diagnose, Direct):

Correction template
In your answer, [what is wrong]. Change it to [what it should be], and keep everything else the same.
Stuck or out of time? Show an example critical prompt

Only open this after you've written your own, then compare.

Example critical prompt
Role: You are a site coordinator handling shift swap requests.
Context: Use the requests, roster, site rules and my answers above.
Task: For each request, decide whether to approve, decline or put it on hold. Show the updated roster for anything approved. Draft a message to each person affected.
Format: A short decision table with the request, the decision, the reason and the site rule it relies on. Then the updated roster as a table. Then one message per person: a short email reply for anyone who emailed, and a brief, friendly chat message for anyone who used WhatsApp.
Constraints: British spelling. Only use facts from the datasets and my answers. Do not assume anyone has agreed unless they said so. Put a request on hold if any question is still open, and say what is needed. Name the site rule behind every decision. No message may promise a swap that hasn't been approved.

If yours is stronger in places, good. There's no single right prompt.

If you get stuck

Try these in order. Only move to the next one if you need it.

  1. Imagine it's crew change day and the swaps have happened. Walk through the site role by role. Is anyone missing?
  2. Go back to your Step 2 table. Is everything in your "What do we still need?" column dealt with?
  3. Which Module 1 technique turns the AI's guesses into questions? Where would it help here?

Tick each one you can honestly say yes to.

  • I tested each request against every site rule before deciding.
  • After any approved change, every role still meets the minimum on site.
  • Nothing is approved on behalf of someone who hasn't agreed.
  • Anything with an open question is on hold, with the question stated.
  • Each decision names the rule it relies on.
  • Each person gets a message suited to them, and none promises more than has been decided.
  • The output meets the quality checklist I set in Step 1.
  • I challenged at least one thing the AI said.

What to take away

  • Test before you act. Ask the AI "if we do this, what breaks?" before you approve anything.
  • Give the AI the rules. It can only check against rules you've given it. Rules it invents are not your rules.
  • Nobody has agreed until they say so. AI readily assumes the other person is on board.
  • Make the consequences visible. A coverage dashboard shows in seconds what takes minutes to reason through.
  • "On hold" is a decision. It's better than a yes you have to take back.
  • One decision, several audiences. Each person needs their own message, in the channel they used.
