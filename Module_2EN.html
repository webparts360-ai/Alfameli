Lab Exercise 2: Prompting Engineering

Every prompt in this lab follows RCTF+C, five parts in this order. The examples below use an email triage task.

  • Role tells the AI whose voice to use and who the reader is. Example: You are an executive assistant to an operations manager at Alfameli.
  • Context gives the situation and why the work matters. Example: The manager receives about 40 emails each morning and has 15 minutes to decide what needs attention first.
  • Task states exactly what to produce. Example: Read the emails above and sort each one into Urgent, Reply this week or For information only.
  • Format sets the shape of the result. Example: A table with sender, subject, category and a one-line reason.
  • Constraints (the +C) set the limits and the rules that must not be broken. Example: Treat anything from a client or about safety as Urgent. Do not draft replies. Use only what is in the emails.

Why it works: the AI answers only from what you give it. Wherever your prompt is silent, it fills the gap with a safe, generic guess. That is where vague, invented or off-target text comes from. Each part of RCTF+C closes one of those gaps, so the first draft needs fewer corrections. In practice Context, Format and Constraints tend to make the biggest difference. Role mostly sets tone and audience, so do not expect it to make the AI more accurate.

Four everyday tasks from Alfameli's work. For each one: understand the job, review the data, run the structured prompt, then debrief.

How to use this lab

  1. Read the Task to understand your role and what the result is for.
  2. Copy the Data and paste it into your AI tool.
  3. Copy the Structured Prompt and paste it under the data, in the same message. Notice how each line maps to RCTF+C.
  4. Check the result against Expected output, then run Prompt 2 in the same conversation.
  5. Compare with the Sample answers and discuss.
Exercise 1Technique: Draft, Diagnose, Direct

Client Reporting

Task

You are a client account manager at Alfameli. The Hess Malaysia client expects a weekly update on how their site is running. Turn the rough site log into a short report that is ready to send, without adding anything that is not in the log.

Data

A rough log from the site supervisor. It is written for you to work from, not for the client to read. Watch for one open item that is easy to leave out: the manning schedule.

Site log, Hess Malaysia contract, week of 8-14 Sept.
Crew: 22 catering staff, full strength all week.
Tue 10 Sept: minor generator fault in dry store, backup power engaged within 15 min, no food spoilage.
Fri 13 Sept: new supervisor Ahmad started orientation.
Client asked twice this week about next month's manning schedule, not yet sent.

Structured Prompt

Paste the data first, then this prompt, in the same message.

Role: You are a client account manager at Alfameli.
Context: This is the weekly update the Hess Malaysia client relies on for visibility into site operations.
Task: Using the site log above, write the weekly update covering crew status, any incidents and any open items the client is waiting on.
Format: One-page report with clear headings.
Constraints: Formal tone, British spelling, no more than 200 words. Use only facts from the log.

Expected output

  • Crew status and the 10 Sept incident, including the action taken
  • The new supervisor mentioned
  • The manning schedule acknowledged as an open item
  • Formal tone, no more than 200 words, nothing beyond the log

Prompt 2

The first report usually says only that the schedule will be sent "shortly". Spot that weakness, then give one precise instruction instead of asking for the whole report again. Expected result: one revised line that commits to 3 working days, with no invented excuse.

The manning schedule has now been outstanding for two weeks and the client has asked twice. Revise the outstanding items line to commit to sending it within 3 working days, without inventing a reason for the delay.
Sample answers
After the Structured Prompt
Weekly Site Update, Hess Malaysia Contract
Week of 8-14 September

Crew remained at full strength throughout the week, with 22 catering staff deployed.

On 10 September, a minor generator fault occurred in the dry store. Backup power was engaged within 15 minutes and no food spoilage resulted.

Ahmad, our new site supervisor, began orientation on 13 September.

We understand next month's manning schedule is outstanding on our end and will be sent shortly.
After Prompt 2
We recognise next month's manning schedule remains outstanding despite your recent enquiries. We will provide this within three working days.
Discussion
What the AI did: it summarised rough notes and chose what matters to the client. With Prompt 2 it corrected one sentence instead of rewriting the whole report.

Question: Check every sentence in both outputs against the data. Is anything there that was not in the log or in your instruction?

Exercise 2Technique: Failure Anticipator

Shift and Site Coordination

Task

You are a site coordinator for a small offshore catering crew. Each week you prepare the roster. It must respect leave requests and the minimum daily coverage required by the client contract. Missing the minimum on even one day is a contract compliance issue.

Data

Eight staff, two leave requests and one firm rule. Watch the minimum: a roster can look right and quietly break it.

8 catering staff: Ali, Bala, Chen, Devi, Farah, Goh, Hafiz, Indra.
Leave requests: Bala off Mon-Tue, Farah off Thu only.
Minimum 5 staff required per day.

Structured Prompt

Paste the data first, then this prompt, in the same message.

Role: You are a site coordinator at Alfameli.
Context: The client contract requires minimum daily coverage. This is a compliance obligation, not a preference.
Task: Using the crew list and leave requests above, build a roster for Monday to Friday.
Format: A table showing who is rostered each day and the daily headcount.
Constraints: At least 5 staff rostered every day, without exception. Respect every leave request.

Expected output

  • A roster table for Monday to Friday
  • Bala's and Farah's leave respected
  • Daily headcount shown, never below 5

Prompt 2

A roster that is safe today can fail with one more change. Test it with a new situation: Chen also asks for Wednesday off. Expected result: an updated roster and an explicit statement on whether minimum coverage still holds every day.

Chen has just requested Wednesday off as well. Rebuild the roster and confirm explicitly whether minimum coverage still holds on every day.

Discussion
What the AI did: it turned a plain list into a table and checked the result against the minimum coverage rule. With Prompt 2 it tested the plan under a new condition. It may also show which days are now tightest, which the first roster did not report because nobody asked.

Question: Remove the "at least 5" rule from the Structured Prompt and run it again. Does the roster change? That shows whether the AI applied the rule or only happened to meet it.

Exercise 3Technique: Trade-off Navigator

Procurement and Logistics

Task

You are a procurement officer sourcing crane wire rope for a project. Three suppliers have quoted and you must recommend one to your manager. The cheapest quote is not automatically the best choice. Your manager will want a reason, not just a number.

Data

Three quotes for the same item. Each supplier is strong on a different factor, so no answer is obvious by itself.

Three quotes for crane wire rope, 20mm, 500m.
Supplier A: RM 18,500, 3-week lead time, 12-month warranty.
Supplier B: RM 16,200, 6-week lead time, 6-month warranty.
Supplier C: RM 19,800, 2-week lead time, 24-month warranty.

Structured Prompt

Paste the data first, then this prompt, in the same message.

Role: You are a procurement officer at Alfameli.
Context: This wire rope is for an active crane operation. The choice affects both cost and schedule.
Task: Compare the three quotes above on price, lead time and warranty, then recommend one supplier.
Format: A comparison table, followed by a short recommendation.
Constraints: Weigh all three factors, not price alone. Give the reason in one sentence.

Expected output

  • A comparison table of price, lead time and warranty
  • One recommended supplier with a one-sentence reason
  • A reason that weighs all three factors, not price alone

Prompt 2

A recommendation made without a deadline can collapse when one appears. Add a new fact: the crane operation cannot proceed past 3 weeks from today, and a delayed delivery halts the project at about RM 8,000 per day. Expected result: a recommendation that genuinely changes, with the reasoning shown, not just different wording.

The engineering team has now confirmed the crane operation cannot proceed past 3 weeks from today. A delayed delivery halts the project at approximately RM 8,000 per day. Does your recommendation change?
Sample answers
After the Structured Prompt
SupplierPrice (RM)Lead timeWarranty
A18,5003 weeks12 months
B16,2006 weeks6 months
C19,8002 weeks24 months
Recommendation: Supplier A offers the best balance, a mid-range price with a reasonable lead time and solid warranty coverage. Supplier B's savings come with the weakest warranty and the longest wait, and Supplier C's speed and warranty come at the highest cost.
After Prompt 2
Yes. With a 3-week hard deadline, Supplier B is no longer viable. Its 6-week lead time would delay the project by about three weeks at roughly RM 8,000 per day, which far exceeds its RM 2,300 saving over Supplier A. Between the other two, Supplier A arrives at exactly 3 weeks with no buffer, while Supplier C's 2-week lead time leaves a week in hand, worth the RM 1,300 premium given the cost of missing the deadline. The recommendation changes to Supplier C.
Discussion
What the AI did: it weighed price against lead time against warranty and made a judgement. A real comparison should change when an important fact changes. That is how you tell reasoning from a template.

Question: Was the first recommendation quietly wrong the moment a deadline appeared? What does that teach about stating constraints up front versus checking for them afterwards?

Exercise 4Technique: Fact-check, show the working

HR and Training

Task

You are an HR coordinator tracking HSE certification for offshore crew. A certificate that lapses unnoticed means someone works without valid certification, which is a compliance failure, not an admin slip. This is the riskiest exercise in the lab because a wrong answer has real consequences.

Data

A short certification log with a reference date of 13 October. Two entries have already lapsed. The rest sit close enough to the 60-day boundary that a miscount is easy.

Today's date: 13 October.
HSE certification expiry: Ali 25 Oct, Bala 2 Oct (expired), Chen 15 Dec, Devi 20 Nov, Farah 8 Oct (expired).

Structured Prompt

Paste the data first, then this prompt, in the same message.

Role: You are an HR coordinator at Alfameli responsible for HSE compliance tracking.
Context: A lapsed certification means a crew member works offshore without valid certification, which is a compliance breach.
Task: Using the training log above, list who is already overdue for renewal and who is due within the next 60 days.
Format: Two lists, each sorted by date.
Constraints: Use 13 October as today's date and calculate precisely. Name anyone outside the 60-day window.

Expected output

  • First list: certificates already lapsed, sorted by date
  • Second list: certificates expiring within 60 days, sorted by date
  • Anyone outside the 60-day window named

Prompt 2

A tidy list cannot be checked on its own. Ask the AI to show its date calculation for each person so you can verify it before acting. Expected result: the number of days for each person, and whether it is in the past or still to come.

Show your date calculation for each person, today's date minus or plus their expiry date, so I can verify each one before I act on this.
Sample answers
After the Structured Prompt
Already overdue:
- Bala, expired 2 October
- Farah, expired 8 October

Due within 60 days (by 12 December):
- Ali, 25 October
- Devi, 20 November

Not flagged: Chen, due 15 December, outside the 60-day window.
After Prompt 2
Bala: expired 2 October, 11 days ago (overdue)
Farah: expired 8 October, 5 days ago (overdue)
Ali: due 25 October, 12 days from today (within 60 days)
Devi: due 20 November, 38 days from today (within 60 days)
Chen: due 15 December, 63 days from today (outside the 60-day window)
Discussion
What the AI did: it calculated a 60-day window from the date given and sorted every record against it. With the working shown, you can check each figure yourself instead of trusting the summary.

Question: Count from 13 October to 15 December yourself (18 days left in October, 30 in November, 15 in December, 63 in total). Are all five calculations right? If one is wrong, what do you do?

Sample answers were written as a reference and were not captured from live runs, so your results will differ a little each time. The pattern matters more than the exact wording.
